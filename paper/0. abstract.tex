\begin{abstract}
Quantitative structure--activity relationship (QSAR) models for Ames mutagenicity are increasingly used in ICH M7 assessments,\cite{ICH_M7_2017} yet reported performance varies greatly depending on evaluation methodology. Here we present a systematic evaluation of 375 model configurations across five genotoxicity datasets---Ames mutagenicity ($n = 13{,}560$), in~vitro chromosome aberration ($n = 1{,}619$), in~vivo micronucleus ($n = 2{,}153$), and two negative-sampled variants---using seven algorithms, five feature representations, and three preprocessing scenarios. Using a single locked configuration (XGBoost, compact descriptors, unprocessed SMILES) evaluated at three levels of increasing stringency, we show that for Ames, performance decreased from random-split cross-validation (MCC = 0.670) to scaffold-split cross-validation (0.624, $\Delta = 0.046$) to cross-source-domain leave-domain-out in the drug-to-industrial direction (0.234). Source-domain confounding---arising from the mixing of drug-like compounds (positive rate 58\%) and industrial chemicals (positive rate 3\%)---drove a scaffold-to-LDO MCC drop of $\Delta = 0.390$, substantially exceeding the split-method effect. A naive source-domain classifier achieved MCC = 0.608 without any structural information. However, algorithm robustness under domain shift was not uniform: Random Forest maintained MCC = 0.325 in the most challenging cross-domain direction where XGBoost collapsed to 0.122. In~vivo micronucleus prediction showed some ranking ability (ROC-AUC = 0.860, PR-AUC = 0.237) after salt stripping but lacked reliable classification utility (MCC 95\% CI crossing zero). We propose multi-level evaluation with source-domain awareness as a reporting standard for Ames mutagenicity QSAR and provide an open-source pipeline for reproducibility.
\end{abstract}
